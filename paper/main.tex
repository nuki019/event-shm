\title{Event-Based Guided Wave Structural Health Monitoring:\
Separating Temperature Drift from Damage by the Temporal Structure\
of Send-on-Delta Event Streams}
\author{Anonymous}
\date{\today}

\begin{document}

\begin{abstract}
Long-term guided-wave structural health monitoring (SHM) produces vast
continuous data streams although ``nothing happens'' most of the time.
We introduce \emph{send-on-delta} (SoD) event-based acquisition to
guided-wave SHM for the first time, at two levels: (A) level-crossing
events of the within-record compensated residual, and (B) SoD events of
the across-record damage-index series. Temperature drift and damage
imprint fundamentally different spatio-temporal event patterns---slow,
globally coherent versus fast, path-localized bursts---which enables
discrimination \emph{without explicit temperature compensation}.
On the public OGW CFRP temperature dataset we show that classical
baseline compensation degrades sharply outside its temperature range
($7.0	imes$ residual inflation for healthy records), and that
level-crossing eventization of the compensated residual retains the
damage-scattering transient at $\leq 1\%$ of the raw data rate,
preserving coarse localization (error $31$~mm) where matched-rate
uniform decimation degrades ($54$~mm). On 4.5 years of outdoor
aluminum-plate data, level-B eventization of the damage-index series
compresses the stream $43	imes$ while keeping temperature-driven
events globally coherent across paths---the structural signature that
separates temperature drift from damage. [Numbers being finalized.]
\end{abstract}
