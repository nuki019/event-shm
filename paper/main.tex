\title{Event-Based Guided Wave Structural Health Monitoring:\
Separating Temperature Drift from Damage by the Temporal Structure\
of Send-on-Delta Event Streams}
\author{Anonymous}
\date{\today}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Long-term guided-wave structural health monitoring (SHM) produces vast
continuous data streams although ``nothing happens'' most of the time.
We introduce \emph{send-on-delta} (SoD) event-based acquisition to
guided-wave SHM for the first time, at two levels: (A) level-crossing
events of the within-record compensated residual, and (B) SoD events of
the across-record damage-index series. Temperature drift and damage
imprint fundamentally different spatio-temporal event patterns---slow,
globally coherent versus fast, path-localized bursts---enabling
discrimination \emph{without explicit temperature compensation}.
On the public OGW CFRP dataset we show that classical compensation
inflates healthy residuals $7.0\times$ outside its temperature
range, and that level-crossing eventization retains detection of weak
surface-disc damage at $\leq 10^{-3}$ of the raw data rate
(D04 AUC $1.000$ at $\approx 5000\times$ compression), beating
matched-rate uniform decimation and SVD/PCA compression
($0.950$ vs.\ $0.825$). The event stream's structure alone separates
temperature from damage (CV AUC $0.814$) and carries a directional
signature (CV $0.69$). On 4.5 years of outdoor aluminum data, level-B
eventization compresses the stream while keeping temperature events
globally coherent. Event-based acquisition is a viable, complementary
alternative to temperature compensation and post-hoc compression for
resource-constrained long-term guided-wave monitoring.
\end{abstract}
