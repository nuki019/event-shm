\subsection{OGW codec data}
The codec audit uses the record subset specified by the frozen protocol from
the Open Guided Waves (OGW) temperature resource \cite{moll2019ogw}. The
available conditions are one CFRP plate under healthy and reversible disc
states; consequently, paths within a record are features rather than
independent observations, and the data do not represent multiple independent
structures. The protocol fixes healthy training dates from 2018-12-13 through
2018-12-17 (168 records), healthy validation dates from 2018-12-18 through
2018-12-19 (73 records), and healthy test dates from 2018-12-20 through
2018-12-22 (81 records). D04 and D24 are held-out damage conditions with 161
records each. The baseline pool is selected only from the healthy training
dates. The resulting comparison is therefore a within-plate applicability
boundary, not a structural replication.

\subsection{Long-term alarm data}
The cold-start audit uses the selected months and local record index defined
by the frozen protocol from the long-term guided-wave dataset
\cite{yang2025dataset}. March 2021 supplies 15,554 healthy calibration
records. April 2021 supplies 15,069 future records; its labelled change
begins at source index 8,401 at 2021-04-17T17:32:16. This is one observed
transition, not a collection of independent failures. The April temperature
support check also finds 831 of 15,069 records (5.5\%) outside the March
calibration temperature range. Consequently, the replay describes threshold
transfer across these two months, not a field false-alarm calibration or a
population detection study.

\paragraph{Scope.}
The two data sources support a software representation and replay audit only.
They do not establish performance on unseen installations, sensors,
materials, damage morphologies, field interference, or hardware platforms.
