\paragraph{A substantive negative codec result.}
The strict comparison yields a clear decision within its scope: bounded SoD
does not lead held-out record AUC among the four tested implementations under
the declared per-record capacity contract. This is not an absence of a
conclusion. The conclusion follows because the same date-separated protocol,
hard-cap accounting, record-level metric, and all-capacity reporting are
applied to each codec, yet SoD is lower in held-out AUC at every capacity for
both D04 and D24. It would be incorrect to restore the historical
matched-count comparison as a headline because sample counts do not charge
SoD timestamp framing or establish parity with general codecs.

\paragraph{What the alarm audit says.}
The cold-start replay cannot establish operational readiness. Across both
frozen feature grids, false calls remain nonzero on the future healthy prefix,
the first new post-onset alarm is delayed by roughly 42.6--43.1 hours, and
post-onset record exceedance is sparse. Reporting this joint outcome is more
informative than reporting one threshold with the smallest false-call rate or
the largest coverage. It does not establish a calibrated field false-alarm
rate, and it does not estimate probability of detection from a single
transition.

\paragraph{Evidence eligibility and non-combination.}
The codec and alarm audits answer different questions with different data
sources. They jointly delimit the claims that are not supported in this
setting, but they do not identify one physical failure mechanism or form an
independent replication pair. Historical exploratory comparisons and
invalidated mechanism-chain artifacts are deliberately excluded rather than
added as favourable or unfavourable observations. Treating an integrity or
runner failure as a codec-performance measurement would conflate evidence
eligibility with the scientific question.

\paragraph{Evaluation boundaries.}
The codec result is tied to a post-compensation software representation, one
CFRP plate with reversible disc conditions, two damage labels, and the
implemented codec families. The April replay has a 5.5\% temperature-support
gap relative to March and one labelled onset. None of those data measure
acquisition-side sampling, embedded memory, energy, real-time latency, sensor
ageing, packet loss, or industrial interference. The reported PCA model sizes
also caution against interpreting a per-record payload alone as a full
deployment cost.

\paragraph{Next evidence.}
The next defensible step is a preregistered multi-structure study with
independent calibration and future test periods, the same hard-cap accounting,
and predeclared alert criteria. To support a mechanism claim, it would also
need an independently eligible confirmation source and a frozen analysis that
separates representation loss from score construction. A hardware claim would
additionally require measured firmware memory, throughput, energy, and
end-to-end latency. Those experiments could change the scope of the
conclusion, but they cannot be inferred from the present replay.
