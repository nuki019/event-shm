\paragraph{Temperature compensation and residual monitoring.}
Temperature compensation is a documented part of guided-wave SHM
\cite{croxford2010}. This paper does not propose a new compensation method:
all codec inputs are post-compensation residuals. The role of compensation in
the present work is a fixed upstream condition that must be isolated from
claims about event representation.

\paragraph{Event reporting.}
SoD is an event-based data-reporting strategy described by Miskowicz
\cite{miskowicz2006}. Its use here does not imply a guided-wave-specific
mechanism or a hardware saving. It is treated as one packetized software
codec whose timestamp gaps, level changes, and configuration bytes are made
explicit.

\paragraph{Compression comparisons.}
Guided-wave compression and denoising have also been studied with
unsupervised sparse and low-rank representations \cite{yang2023compression}. Rather than
arguing that a sparse representation should replace those approaches, the
strict experiment compares SoD directly with a uniform interpolation codec,
PCA, and Haar discrete wavelet coding under the same record-level capacity
contract. The resulting comparison is limited to the frozen data split and
the four implemented codecs.
