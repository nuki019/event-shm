We introduced send-on-delta event-based acquisition to guided-wave SHM,
with two-level eventization (within-record level-crossing events and
across-record damage-index events). The central findings are: (i)
classical temperature compensation is fragile outside its baseline range
($7.0\times$ residual inflation on healthy records), motivating
compensation-free structure; (ii) SoD eventization retains the
damage-scattering transient at $\leq 10^{-3}$ of the raw data rate,
preserving detection (D04 AUC $1.000$ at $\approx 5000\times$
compression) and coarse structure where matched-rate uniform decimation
and SVD/PCA compression degrade (event $0.950$ vs.\ PCA $0.825$ at
rate $2\times10^{-3}$); and (iii) the temporal structure of the
event stream---globally coherent for temperature, path-localized for
damage---separates the two without a learned temperature model
(CV AUC $0.814$) and yields a measurable directional signature
(CV $0.69$). Event-based acquisition is thus a viable and complementary
alternative to both temperature compensation and post-hoc compression
for long-term, resource-constrained guided-wave monitoring. Making the
long-term level-B channel robust to cross-month sensor drift---e.g.\ by
coupling it with drift-robust references---is the main direction for
future work.
